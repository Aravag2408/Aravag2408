Some things I've worked on:

**AchvaLens** · [live app](https://achvalens-b7f0htgxhyc7fsbt.israelcentral-01.azurewebsites.net/app)  
Our team built this for Achva LGBT, a nonprofit. You paste in an academic text and it marks
LGBTQ+ terminology as fine, worth reconsidering, or harmful. The hardest category was the middle
one. Automated prompt optimization pushed overall accuracy from 63.6% to 69.8%.
The code is private.

**[Mercury](https://github.com/Aravag2408/mercury-audit)** · [live demo](https://mercury-audit.onrender.com)  
A privacy audit of a medical chatbot. I found that a stranger's private health details could
end up in your answer, just by asking a similar question. In my test, 18 of 20 fictional
patients' details reached the model, and 6 showed up in replies. I traced it to one missing
check and mapped the fixes to GDPR.

**[SugarBuddy Agent](https://github.com/Aravag2408/SugerBuddy_Agent)** · [live demo](https://suger-buddy-agent.vercel.app)  
For parents of teens with Type 1 diabetes. When a glucose reading looks wrong, the agent asks
the teen a few quick questions and gives the parent a calm summary of what probably happened,
instead of an alarm.

**State, Not Trait** (research, private)  
Do people hold steady values, or does it depend on how you ask? I analyzed 42 players of a
democracy simulation game, and the answer was mostly "it depends". Along the way I built an LLM
grader for 480 written answers and checked it against two human experts. The best version agreed with them
about as well as they agreed with each other.
